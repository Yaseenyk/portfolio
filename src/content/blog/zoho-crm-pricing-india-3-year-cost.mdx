**Zoho CRM costs ₹800 to ₹2,600 per user per month in India depending on the edition, billed yearly and before GST. For a team of 10 on the Standard edition that is ₹2,88,000 over three years.** The three-year number is the one worth knowing, because a CRM is not a purchase you reverse in a quarter. This post prices it from Zoho's own figures, then says plainly when a custom CRM is worth a quote and when it is not.

I build custom software for a living, so read the next sentence with that in mind: for most small teams with a standard sales process, Zoho is the right answer. The rest of this post is about finding out whether you are one of those teams.

## What Zoho CRM costs per user in India

These are the rupee prices on Zoho's pricing page, per user per month, before GST. I read them on 23 October 2026. Prices change, so check the page and your final invoice before you decide.

| Edition | Billed yearly | Billed monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | ₹800 | ₹1,300 |
| Professional | ₹1,400 | ₹2,100 |
| Enterprise | ₹2,400 | see Zoho's page |
| Ultimate | ₹2,600 | see Zoho's page |

I have left the Enterprise and Ultimate monthly prices out on purpose. Sources that republish Zoho's prices disagree on those two figures, and a number I cannot pin down does not belong in a cost comparison.

There is also a free edition. Third-party listings, including [Capterra India](https://www.capterra.in/software/155928/zoho-crm), describe it as free for up to 3 users. If your sales team is two or three people, start there and do not read the rest of this post as a reason to spend money.

Zoho's page also lists a team-user add-on at ₹400 per user per month billed yearly, or ₹500 billed monthly. I am not going to describe what it includes, because that is Zoho's to say and the details change. If a plan you are considering needs an add-on, add it to the sums below.

## The three-year cost for a real team

Monthly prices hide the scale of the commitment. Here is the same data as a three-year total, which is the price per user, times the number of users, times 36 months. These use the yearly-billing prices and exclude GST.

| Edition | 5 users | 10 users | 25 users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | ₹1,44,000 | ₹2,88,000 | ₹7,20,000 |
| Professional | ₹2,52,000 | ₹5,04,000 | ₹12,60,000 |
| Enterprise | ₹4,32,000 | ₹8,64,000 | ₹21,60,000 |

Two things stand out.

First, the edition matters more than the headcount for a small team. Five people on Enterprise cost about three times what five people on Standard do. Whether you need the higher edition is a question about your process, not your size, and you should answer it with a trial before you pay for it.

Second, the cost grows in a straight line with every salesperson you hire. That is fine when the tool fits and is the part to watch when it does not, because you pay the same per seat whether or not you use the features you are paying for.

## Billing monthly costs more than most people expect

Everyone compares editions. Fewer people compare billing periods. Paying monthly instead of yearly costs 62.5% more on Standard: ₹1,300 against ₹800 per user per month. On a 25-user team that gap, over a year, is a large amount of money for the privilege of being able to leave.

If you are confident you will still be using the CRM in twelve months, billing yearly is the cheapest decision on this page. If you are not confident, that is a signal to run a free trial or the free edition first and commit only when the process is settled.

## When a custom CRM starts to compete

A custom CRM is software built around your own sales process, which you own outright. My published starting price for an AI-integrated CRM built this way is $2,000, one time, with you owning the code. Smaller pieces of work are $15 to $30 an hour. A real quote depends on your scope, so treat those as the starting point and not a price for your team.

To compare it with the tables above, convert the dollar figure at the day's exchange rate and then add what the build price leaves out. A custom CRM has running costs too: hosting, maintenance, and above all someone who owns it and can change it when the process changes. That last cost is the one people forget, and it is the reason custom software loses to a subscription more often than it wins.

A custom build is worth pricing in three situations.

- **Your process is not standard.** If your pipeline has stages, approvals or hand-offs that a general CRM can only imitate with workarounds, you are paying for a seat licence and then paying again, in staff time, to bend the tool.
- **Your team is large and stable.** Seat pricing is a recurring cost that scales with headcount, while a build is mostly a one-off cost. The bigger and steadier the team, the more the per-seat total on the table above looks like a down payment.
- **The CRM is not the whole job.** If the real need is a CRM plus WhatsApp follow-ups, document generation or an AI step in the middle of the process, a bespoke system can do all of it in one place instead of stitching three subscriptions together.

If none of those describes you, you are in the large group for whom the subscription wins. I made the same argument from the other side in [the post on renting a WhatsApp chatbot versus owning one](/blog/whatsapp-chatbot-saas-vs-owning-it/): renting is the sensible default, and owning is worth it only when your situation actually breaks the default.

## A quick way to decide

Run this check before you spend anything:

1. Count the people who will use the CRM daily, not the people who might look at it.
2. Write down your sales process as a list of stages, in plain words, on one page.
3. Try the free edition, or a trial of the lowest edition, and run two weeks of real leads through it.
4. Note every step where you had to work around the tool, and how long each workaround took.

If the list from step four is short, buy the subscription, billed yearly, and move on. If it is long and the same few steps keep recurring, you now have a specification for a custom build, which is the useful by-product of doing the exercise at all.

## The short version

Zoho CRM is good value for a team with a standard process, and the free edition covers the smallest teams. Price it over three years, not one month, avoid monthly billing if you can commit, and add GST and any add-ons to the figures above. A custom CRM is worth a quote when your process is unusual, your team is large and stable, or the CRM is one part of a bigger workflow.
