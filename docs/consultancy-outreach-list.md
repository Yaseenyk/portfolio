# Staffing consultancies that place full-stack developers

Compiled 2026-10-21. Every firm here is **employer-paid** — the client company
pays the fee, not the candidate. None of them should ever ask you for money.

**Every link below was verified by fetching the site on 2026-10-21.** Where a
page published only a relative path, the full URL is that path on the firm's own
domain. Where I could not reach a site, it says so rather than guessing a URL.

Email addresses appear only where the firm actually publishes one. Most
recruitment firms deliberately do not publish a candidate inbox — they route
through a CV upload so the submission lands in their ATS. That is the channel
they search when a mandate comes in, so using it is not a worse option than
email; it is the intended one.

## How to actually use this list

The upload alone rarely produces anything. The sequence matters.

### 1. Register in the ATS

Work down the tables and submit through each firm's own form. Budget an evening
for all of them. This is not the outreach — it is so that when a recruiter
searches their database for "React Node senior", you are a result. Skip this and
a cold email gets a "please upload your CV" reply, which is the one outcome worth
avoiding.

### 2. Email a named recruiter

This is the step that produces interviews.

- Search LinkedIn for the firm name plus your stack: `"Michael Page" recruiter React`
- Pick someone who **posted a full-stack role in the last 30 days**. They have a
  live mandate and a quota.
- Get their address from the **Contact info** panel on their profile, or from the
  job post itself — recruiters put their email in the post constantly.
- Draft with the **"A staffing consultant"** mode at `/outreach`, which produces
  a short note built from the portfolio and links the résumé PDF.

A single email to a person who places your stack beats fifty to a generic inbox.

### 3. Follow up once

Five working days later. One follow-up, then stop and move to the next firm.
Chasing a non-reply past that spends goodwill you may want later.

## Priority order

Given a senior full-stack and AI profile based in India:

1. **Tier 1, all eight.** No local salary band anchors you, they pay in USD, and
   the vetting is a technical test rather than a screening call. Andela and
   Uplers have exact signup URLs, so start there.
2. **Tier 3 next.** They hold the most open MERN mandates in India.
3. **Tier 2 last.** Slowest and most gatekept, but the right channel for a
   permanent role at a large company.

Register with eight to ten across the tiers. Two or three will produce
everything; there is no way to know in advance which.

## Two things to watch

**Never pay a fee.** Michael Page, Adecco, CIEL and Quess all publish scam
warnings about people impersonating their recruiters and asking candidates for
money. A real employer-paid consultancy never invoices the candidate. Quess
publishes `alert@quesscorp.com` specifically for reporting this.

**Check the sender domain.** Mail from these firms comes from their own domain.
An offer from a Gmail address claiming to be a staffing firm is a scam, and these
firms say so themselves.
