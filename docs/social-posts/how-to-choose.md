Your 24-week project may have only 11 working weeks.

That is the first thing to calculate before choosing a final year project topic.

Start with the calendar. Then subtract a four-week buffer. Subtract exam weeks. Subtract placement drives. Subtract internal reviews, documentation days, and the weeks where your team will not work properly.

Many students pick a topic as if they have a full semester. Later, in March, they are still fixing login, dataset issues, model accuracy, or hardware problems.

A better topic is one that fits the time you actually have.

If you have 10–12 real weeks, choose a project where the main module can be built early. Keep the risky part small. Make sure you can explain the architecture, database, API flow, error cases, and limitations in viva.

Before choosing a topic, count properly:

Total weeks left
minus 4-week buffer
minus exam weeks
minus placement drives
minus internal submissions

That number decides your project.

Not the title.
Not the trending tech stack.
Not what your friend picked.

If the remaining time is small, avoid topics that need datasets, hardware delays, paid APIs, or heavy research.

Pick something you can build, explain, debug, and defend in viva.
