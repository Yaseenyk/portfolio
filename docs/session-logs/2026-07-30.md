## What we built

### 1. Final year projects portal (`/final-year-projects`)

A student-facing surface selling built-and-explained final year projects to
BCA/MCA/B.Tech students in India. Static-only — no auth, no payment gateway, no
backend. Enquiries reuse the existing EmailJS contact pipeline so leads land in
the same inbox.

- **Catalog + 5 listings** — `lib/campus.ts` is the single source of truth.
  Listing files under `src/content/campus/` import the TYPE only; a runtime
  import closes a cycle and hits a TDZ error during the static export.
- **6 per-course landing pages** (`/for/{bca,mca,btech,bsc-it,mtech,diploma}`)
  with hand-written copy per course — near-duplicate pages get filtered out of
  the index.
- **6 long-form guides** (`/guides`) — viva questions, topic lists per degree,
  report format, how to choose. Deliberately separate from `lib/blog`: the blog
  is engineering writing for a professional audience, and merging the two would
  dilute both brands and pollute the RAG corpus.
- **2 free tools** — timeline planner (usable weeks after exams and a realistic
  buffer) and an 89-question viva bank with localStorage progress.
- **`/colleges`** — HODs and placement cells are a different buyer: free
  workshop as the entry, cohort allocation as the pitch.
- **`/terms`** — payment, delivery milestones, cancellation, licence.

Conversion work: sticky mobile WhatsApp bar, trust strip linking only to
already-public evidence, a price-objection comparison table that concedes the
row where the cheap option genuinely wins, and an early-bird band that retires
itself when `EARLY_BIRD.seats` hits zero.

### 2. Client services surface (`/solutions`)

B2B and B2C, kept deliberately separate from `/hire` so the recruiter message
and the client message never mix.

- **`/solutions/business`** — architecture review (₹25k–75k), AI integration
  (₹1.5L–6L), fixed-scope builds (₹2L–10L), ERP replacement (₹6L–25L). Each

## Decisions worth remembering

- **No competitor pricing anywhere on `/solutions`.** An unsourced claim about
  what SAP or an agency charges is the one thing on that surface that could be
  challenged. Transparent own-pricing against an unnamed enterprise quote
  persuades just as well.
- **Blog titles dropped the sitewide brand suffix.** Google shows ~60
  characters; the median post title was 73, and `" | Yaseen Khatib"` added 16
  more. 89% of posts had the headline cut mid-sentence. Using `title.absolute`
  on blog posts took truncation from 98/110 to 33/110 and the median from 73 to
  57 characters.
- **AI blogger throttled because quality signals are sitewide.** 100+ posts
  earning little engagement drag down the pages that convert and consume crawl
  budget those pages need. Volume was never the constraint on this domain —
  authority is.
- **D1 binding stays commented out in `wrangler.toml`.** `worker.yml` deploys on
  every push to `worker/**`; a binding pointing at a database that does not
  exist would fail the deploy and take `/api/chat` and `/mcp` down with it.
- **Résumé download links carry a version suffix.** Browsers hold
  `/Resume.pdf` in disk cache and re-serve it on click regardless of what the
  server has. `RESUME_URL` in `lib/site.ts` now carries `?v=N` — bump it after
  every `generate:resume` run.
- **Positioning is done-for-you, not a source licence.** The student writes none
  of it; the sessions exist so they can defend work they did not write. The
  college-disclosure clause in terms stayed unchanged.

## Corrections made during the session

Two claims I made and then had to retract — recorded so the same mistakes are
not repeated:

1. **"The résumé PDF is verified."** The first check searched raw PDF bytes and
   found the address, which only proved a `mailto:` link annotation existed —
   those sit uncompressed. Visible text is font-subset encoded. The second check
   rendered the *generator's HTML*, which proves what the script produces, not
   what is in the file. Only inspecting `public/Resume.pdf` itself was valid.
   **Verify the artifact the user is looking at, not an adjacent one.**
2. **"Search Console was never verified."** Concluded from `google: ""` in
   `lib/site.ts`. That is only the meta-tag method — the site was verified by
   DNS the whole time and had been collecting data. **An empty config slot is
   not evidence a thing is unconfigured.**
